**Weak nucleophiles react by the SN1 mechanism while strong ones react by SN2.**

We can see the changeover from SN1 to SN2 in the reactions of a single compound if we choose one that is good at both mechanisms, such as a benzyl sulfonium salt. Both mechanisms are available for this compound.

Allyl compounds react rapidly by the SN2 mechanism because the double bond can stabilize the transition state by conjugation.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 18.07%; top: 44.77%; width: 79.70%; height: 11.55%;">

The benzyl group acts in much the same way using the π system of the benzene ring for conjugation with the p orbital in the transition state.

</div>

With α-bromo carbonyl compounds, substitution leads to two electrophilic groups on neighbouring carbon atoms. Each has a low-energy empty orbital, π* from C=O and σ* from C–Br, and these can combine to form a molecular LUMO (π* + σ*) lower in energy than either. Nucleophilic attack will occur easily where this new orbital has its largest coefficients, shown in orange on the diagram.
