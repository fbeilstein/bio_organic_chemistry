Bohr effect

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 15.95%; top: 62.55%; width: 84.05%; height: 36.86%;">

Lower pH → more **protonation** of amino acid side chains (mainly histidines).
Protonated histidines form **salt bridges** (electrostatic bonds) with nearby residues.
These stabilize the **T state** (tense, low-affinity state) of hemoglobin.
Thus, O₂ release is favored in acidic conditions.

Additionally regulated with 2,3-BPG, but we will get to that in later lectures.

</div>

</div>

Different versions of hemoglobin

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 21.45%; top: 13.13%; width: 30.03%; height: 53.18%;">

Fetal hemoglobin (HbF) is structurally different from normal adult hemoglobin (HbA), giving HbF a higher affinity for oxygen than HbA. HbF is composed of two alpha and two gamma chains, whereas HbA is composed of two alpha and two beta chains.

</div>

![](./lecture_03_acids_bases/images/slide_74_img_123.png) {left=57.05 top=8.47 width=32.77 height=54.58}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 18.07%; top: 67.01%; width: 80.35%; height: 31.23%;">

**Neonatal jaundice.** In newborns, jaundice tends to develop because of two factors—the breakdown of fetal hemoglobin as it is replaced with adult hemoglobin and the relatively immature metabolic pathways of the liver, which are unable to conjugate and so excrete bilirubin as quickly as an adult. This causes an accumulation of bilirubin in the blood (hyperbilirubinemia), leading to the symptoms of jaundice.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 63.37%; top: 52.37%; width: 33.97%; height: 47.63%;">

The crocodile icefish or white-blooded fish comprise a family (Channichthyidae) of notothenioid fish found in the Southern Ocean around Antarctica. They are the only known vertebrates to lack hemoglobin in their blood as adults.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 18.63%; top: 5.50%; width: 38.60%; height: 89.11%;">

Most fish have multiple hemoglobin types (isohemoglobins) adapted to varying oxygen levels, with different affinities. 

One or more of the isohemoglobins may exhibit the **Root effect**.

Some primitive species, like lampreys, have unique hemoglobin that dissociates into monomers for higher oxygen affinity. 

In contrast, icefish are an exception, with adult forms lacking hemoglobin entirely due to adaptations for cold, oxygen-rich environments.

</div>

</div>

**Evil twin - the Root effect**

</div>

![](./lecture_03_acids_bases/images/slide_76_img_125.png) {left=50.00 top=-0.00 width=50.12 height=73.76}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 20.32%; width: 32.12%; height: 53.44%;">

**Bohr effect:** tunes O₂ delivery in tissues (affinity shift).
**Root effect (fish):** enables *massive O₂ release* under acidic conditions, even when blood is oxygen-rich — crucial for swim bladder inflation and retinal function.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 19.93%; top: 75.47%; width: 80.35%; height: 24.53%;">

It  not only decreases saturation up to atmospheric O2 levels, but also continues to be effective even at extremely high *P*O2 of several hundred atmospheres, severely limiting the O2-carrying capacity of the blood.

</div>

</div>

<div style="position: relative; width: 100%; aspect-ratio: 16 / 9; margin: 0 auto;">

![](./lecture_03_acids_bases/images/slide_77_img_126.png) {left=26.42 top=0.00 width=69.62 height=58.62}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 19.74%; top: 58.62%; width: 76.30%; height: 39.04%;">

The **swim bladder**, gas bladder, fish maw, or air bladder is an internal gas-filled organ in bony fish that functions to modulate buoyancy, and thus allowing the fish to stay at desired water depth without having to maintain lift via swimming, which expends more energy.

The walls of the bladder contain very few blood vessels and are lined with guanine crystals, which make them **impermeable to gases **except for **oval window** and **rete mirabile**.

</div>

</div>

---

<div style="position: relative; width: 100%; aspect-ratio: 16 / 9; margin: 0 auto;">

![](./lecture_03_acids_bases/images/slide_78_img_127.png) {left=21.00 top=0.00 width=68.39 height=62.10}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 14.54%; top: 62.10%; width: 85.46%; height: 37.90%;">

**Going DOWN.** As pressure increases the bladder shrinks and the gas gland has to introduce gas (usually oxygen) to keep its volume constant and thus buoyancy. 

gas gland excretes lactic acid and produces carbon dioxide
CO2 acidifies blood in the rete mirabile via the bicarbonate buffer system. 
Root effect causes the hemoglobin of the blood to lose its oxygen
O2 diffuses partly into the swim bladder.

</div>

</div>

---

<div style="position: relative; width: 100%; aspect-ratio: 16 / 9; margin: 0 auto;">

![](./lecture_03_acids_bases/images/slide_79_img_128.png) {left=35.54 top=-0.00 width=40.64 height=58.41}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 18.81%; top: 58.41%; width: 81.19%; height: 41.59%;">

Before returning to the body, the blood re-enters the rete mirabile, and as a result, virtually all the excess carbon dioxide and oxygen produced in the gas gland diffuses back to the arteries supplying the gas gland via a countercurrent multiplication loop.

A very high gas pressure of oxygen can be obtained, which can even account for the presence of gas in the swim bladders of deep sea fish like the eel, requiring a pressure of hundreds of bars.

</div>

</div>

<div style="position: relative; width: 100%; aspect-ratio: 16 / 9; margin: 0 auto;">

<div style="position: absolute; left: 17.33%; top: 59.55%; width: 82.67%; height: 40.45%;">

**Going UP.** Pressure decreases and bladder expands dangerously. The **oval window** is a specific vascularized pouch on the posterior or dorsal wall of a fish's swim bladder that serves to resorb gases from the bladder, particularly in fish that have lost their pneumatic duct connection. It contains a specialized capillary network where muscle can relax to expose its vascular surface to the gases in the main bladder or constrict to separate it, facilitating the slow, controlled release of gas from the swim bladder to regulate buoyancy.

</div>
