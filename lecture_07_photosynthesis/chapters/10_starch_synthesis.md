<div style="position: absolute; left: 14.17%; top: 2.53%; width: 51.98%; height: 94.06%;">

Main transported sugar in most plants (via **phloem**, not xylem).

Formed in the cytosol from UDP-glucose + fructose-6-phosphate.

Transported from source (photosynthetic tissues) → sink (roots, seeds, fruits).

Non-reducing disaccharide — chemically stable for long-distance transport.

Cleaved by invertase or sucrose synthase for metabolism.

Central hub linking photosynthesis, respiration, and starch synthesis.

</div>

**Xylem**: transports water and minerals from the roots upward. This is what the bulb needs so that when it sprouts, the young leaves can grow and start photosynthesizing.

**Phloem**: transports sugars and other organic molecules, usually from photosynthetic tissues (leaves) to storage organs (like the onion bulb)

<div style="position: absolute; left: 11.01%; top: 0.00%; width: 36.94%; height: 94.06%;">

Starch synthesis occurs in chloroplasts (leaves) and amyloplasts (storage tissues).
ADP-glucose is the activated glucose donor for starch formation.
ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase (AGPase) catalyzes:
           Glc-1-P+ATP→ADP-Glc+PP𝑖
Starch synthases and branching enzymes build amylopectin and amylose chains.
In leaves, starch accumulates during the day and is degraded at night to form sucrose.
Sucrose synthesis occurs in the cytosol via UDP-glucose and fructose-6-phosphate.
Sucrose is exported through phloem to heterotrophic tissues, where it is broken down and converted back to ADP-glucose for storage starch.
In cereal endosperm, AGPase and ADP-glucose synthesis occur mainly in the cytosol (unlike in other plants).

</div>

During germination, the release of the hormone gibberellic acid from the embryo (1) triggers the secretion of enzymes from the living aleurone layer surrounding the starchy endosperm (2) and from the scutellum. Three types of starch‐degrading enzymes are released: α‐amylase, the debranching enzyme limit dextrinase, and α‐glucosidase
(also called maltase). Proteases for degrading storage proteins are also secreted. In addition, secreted proteases activate a preformed β‐amylase stored within the endosperm through limited proteolysis of its carboxyl terminus. Together, these enzymes hydrolyze the starch to glucose and the proteins to free amino acids (3)

**One more sugar. Cellulose structure. **

The plant cell wall is made up in part of cellulose molecules arranged side by side to form crystalline arrays—cellulose microfibrils. Several microfibrils may combine to form larger  cellulose macrofibrils. The scanning electron microscope shows macrofibrils, 5 to 12 nm
in diameter, laid down on the cell surface in several layers distinguishable by the different orientations of the fibrils.

**Cellulose**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 24.12%; width: 80.35%; height: 57.99%;">

Polymer of glucose (repeating β-D-glucose units).
Monomers linked by β(1→4) glycosidic bonds (not α as in starch).
Forms long, straight chains that associate via hydrogen bonds → microfibrils.
Synthesized at the plasma membrane by cellulose synthase complexes (CesA) using UDP-glucose as the substrate.
Provides structural strength and rigidity to the plant cell wall.

</div>
