<div style="position: absolute; left: 15.83%; top: 12.64%; width: 84.17%; height: 87.36%;">

**Place of Synthesis:**
**Hypothalamic**: TRH, GnRH, CRH, GHRH, somatostatin, dopamine (neurotransmitter-like).
**Pituitary (Anterior):** GH, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH, prolactin.
**Pituitary (Posterior):** Vasopressin (ADH), oxytocin.
Central regulators of endocrine homeostasis — “master controllers.”
Act through specific target glands (thyroid, adrenal, gonads).
Mostly peptide hormones, short half-life, require receptor-mediated signaling.
Clinical relevance:
GH deficiency/excess: dwarfism vs. acromegaly.
ACTH: excess → Cushing’s disease; deficiency → adrenal insufficiency.
ADH deficiency: diabetes insipidus; analog desmopressin used therapeutically.
Oxytocin: induces labor, milk ejection; used clinically for uterine stimulation.
Drug analogs: somatropin (GH), octreotide (somatostatin analog), triptorelin (GnRH analog).

**Steroid Hormones**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 14.46%; width: 83.93%; height: 83.63%;">

Derived from cholesterol, produced in adrenal cortex, gonads, and placenta.
Slow-acting genomic effects, long duration.
Clinical roles:
Glucocorticoids (e.g., cortisol): anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressive → prednisone, dexamethasone.
Mineralocorticoids (e.g., aldosterone): sodium retention → regulated by renin-angiotensin system.
Sex steroids: testosterone, estrogen, progesterone → used in hormone replacement, contraception, oncology.
Adverse effects: Cushingoid features, osteoporosis, metabolic syndrome (for glucocorticoids).

</div>

</div>

**Thyroid Hormones (T₃ & T₄)**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 24.12%; width: 80.35%; height: 57.99%;">

Regulate basal metabolic rate, growth, CNS development.
Long-acting, lipophilic hormones with nuclear receptors.
Clinical importance:
Hypothyroidism: fatigue, bradycardia, weight gain → treated with levothyroxine (T₄).
Hyperthyroidism: tachycardia, tremor, weight loss → treated with methimazole, propylthiouracil.
Iodine deficiency → goiter formation.
Drug interactions: amiodarone, lithium, corticosteroids can affect thyroid function

</div>

</div>

**Catecholamines**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 23.66%; width: 80.35%; height: 52.67%;">

Derived from tyrosine, act rapidly via adrenergic GPCRs (α, β).
Epinephrine, norepinephrine, dopamine — mediators of acute stress ("fight or flight").
Pharmacology:
Epinephrine: anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest (β₁/β₂ agonist).
Dopamine: used in shock and heart failure (dose-dependent receptor selectivity).
β-blockers: antagonize catecholamine effects (hypertension, arrhythmia).
Metabolic effects: glycogenolysis, lipolysis, increased cardiac output.

</div>

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 0.00%; width: 15.24%; height: 10.56%;">

**Eicosanoids**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.95%; top: 32.22%; width: 80.35%; height: 57.99%;">

Derived from arachidonic acid via COX and LOX pathways.
Extremely short-lived autocrine/paracrine mediators.
Include prostaglandins, thromboxanes, leukotrienes.
Clinical significance:
NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen) inhibit COX → reduce inflammation and pain.
Leukotriene modifiers (montelukast) used in asthma.
Prostaglandin analogs: used in labor induction, glaucoma (latanoprost), gastric protection (misoprostol).
Key role in inflammation, platelet aggregation, vascular tone.

</div>
