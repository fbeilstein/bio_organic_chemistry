**TGF-β / Smad Signal Transduction Pathway**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 24.12%; width: 47.64%; height: 69.84%;">

TGF-β ligand binds a Type II receptor serine/threonine kinase
Type II receptor phosphorylates and activates a Type I receptor
Activated Type I receptor phosphorylates receptor-regulated Smads (R-Smads) — typically Smad2 and Smad3
R-Smads form a complex with Smad4 (co-Smad)
The Smad complex translocates to the nucleus
In the nucleus, Smads bind DNA and transcriptional co-activators/repressors→ regulation of cell growth, differentiation, and ECM production
Inhibitory Smads (Smad6/7) provide negative feedback by blocking receptor activity

</div>

TGF-β is released as a complex with LAP (Latency-Associated Peptide) and often tethered to the extracellular matrix via LTBP (Latent TGF-β Binding Protein).
The latent complex must be mechanically or proteolytically “unlocked” before TGF-β can bind its receptor.
**Mechanisms of activation:**
Integrins (αvβ6, αvβ8) apply mechanical force to LAP.
Proteases (MMPs, plasmin) or acidic pH cleave LAP.
Occasionally ROS or other local factors contribute.
**Physiological relevance:**
This tight, localized activation prevents unwanted effects such as fibrosis, immune suppression, or excessive growth inhibition.

**Notch/Delta Signaling Pathway**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 11.07%; top: 14.74%; width: 49.24%; height: 81.31%;">

**Contact-dependent signaling:** Both Notch receptor and Delta (or Jagged) ligand are membrane proteins — signaling occurs only between adjacent cells.
**Ligand binding and cleavage:** When Delta binds Notch, the receptor’s extracellular domain is cleaved by ADAM protease, then further cut by γ-secretase.
**Release of Notch intracellular domain (NICD):** The NICD fragment enters the nucleus.
**Transcriptional activation:** NICD binds the CSL (CBF1/Su(H)/Lag-1) transcription factor, converting it from a repressor to an activator. → Induces genes controlling cell differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis.

</div>

![](./lecture_08_molecular_signaling/images/slide_75_img_96.png) {left=62.81 top=-0.00 width=36.35 height=54.31}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 60.31%; top: 58.06%; width: 38.85%; height: 40.36%;">

**Biological significance:**
Determines cell fate decisions (e.g. lateral inhibition in neurogenesis).
Involved in stem-cell maintenance and tissue patterning.
Dysregulation linked to cancer and developmental disorders.

</div>

**Wnt/β-Catenin Signaling Pathway**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 13.55%; width: 37.22%; height: 84.28%;">

**Ligand and receptor: **Wnt proteins (secreted glycoproteins) bind to Frizzled (Fz) receptors and LRP5/6 co-receptors on the cell surface.

**Without Wnt: **β-catenin is continuously degraded by the “destruction complex” (Axin, APC, GSK3β, CK1). → Low cytosolic β-catenin → Target genes repressed by TCF/LEF.

**With Wnt: **Binding of Wnt to Fz/LRP inactivates the destruction complex via Dishevelled (Dsh) activation.→ β-catenin accumulates and enters the nucleus.

**Nuclear action: **β-catenin binds TCF/LEF transcription factors, converting them from repressors to activators of gene expression. → Induces genes controlling cell proliferation, differentiation, and fate determination.

</div>

![](./lecture_08_molecular_signaling/images/slide_76_img_97.png) {left=53.30 top=26.24 width=46.71 height=73.76}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 53.29%; top: 1.98%; width: 45.41%; height: 24.26%;">

Essential for embryonic patterning and stem cell renewal.
Dysregulated Wnt signaling → cancer (e.g., colon carcinoma), fibrosis, and developmental defects.

</div>

</div>

**Hedgehog (Hh) Signaling Pathway**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 53.03%; width: 83.13%; height: 46.97%;">

**Ligand and receptors: **Hedgehog (Hh) family ligands — Sonic (Shh), Indian (Ihh), Desert (Dhh) — bind to the Patched (PTCH) receptor on the cell membrane.
**Without Hh: **PTCH inhibits Smoothened (SMO), a GPCR-like protein. GLI transcription factors (GLI2/3) are phosphorylated and processed into repressors (GLI-R).→ Target genes remain silent.
**With Hh present: **Hh binding relieves PTCH inhibition on SMO. Activated SMO initiates signaling that prevents GLI cleavage. Full-length GLI activators (GLI-A) accumulate and enter the nucleus.→ Transcription of Hh-responsive genes (e.g., involved in proliferation and patterning).

</div>

![](./lecture_08_molecular_signaling/images/slide_77_img_98.png) {left=51.13 top=-0.00 width=48.87 height=53.03}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 11.74%; top: 20.67%; width: 38.26%; height: 31.43%;">

Critical for embryonic patterning, neural tube, and limb development.
Aberrant activation → cancers (basal cell carcinoma, medulloblastoma).

</div>

</div>

**NF-κB signaling pathway**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 11.74%; top: 50.35%; width: 87.26%; height: 50.00%;">

**Inactive state: **NF-κB is bound to IκB, sequestered in the cytoplasm → no transcription
**Activation:** Triggered by cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1), pathogen signals (via TLRs), or stress. IKK complex phosphorylates IκB, marking it for ubiquitination and degradation. Freed NF-κB translocates to the nucleus.
**Nuclear action: **NF-κB binds DNA and activates target genes for inflammation (e.g., cytokines, COX-2), cell survival (anti-apoptotic genes), immune responses.
**Termination / feedback: **NF-κB induces IκB gene transcription, which re-inhibits NF-κB → negative feedback loop

</div>

<div style="position: relative; width: 100%; aspect-ratio: 16 / 9; margin: 0 auto;">

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 3.81%; width: 80.35%; height: 20.31%;">

**Signalosomes **are large supramolecular protein complexes that undergo clustering (oligomerisation or polymerisation) and/or colloidal phase separation to form biomolecular condensates that increase the local concentration and signalling activity of the individual components. They are an example of molecular self-assembly and self-organisation in cell biology.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 24.12%; width: 80.35%; height: 71.81%;">

**Wnt signalosome:** Transduction of Wnt signals from the plasma membrane depends on clustering of LRP6 receptors with Dishevelled (Dvl) proteins to recruit the Axin complex for inactivation.

**B-cell receptor (BCR) signalosome:** The B-cell receptor (BCR) binds antigen and undergoes clustering to induce signal transduction.

**T-cell receptor (TCR) signalosome:** Antigen presentation to T-cells is recognised by the T-cell receptor (TCR), which initiates clustering and activation of downstream signalling to induce T-cell responses.

**COP9 signalosome:** Catalyses the hydrolysis of NEDD8 protein from the Cullin subunit of Cullin-RING ubiquitin ligases (CRL). Therefore, it is responsible for CRL deneddylation – at the same time, it is able to bind deneddylated cullin-RING complex and retain them in deactivated form. COP9 signalosome thus serves as a sole deactivator of CRLs.

**RIP1/RIP3 Necrosome:** A signalling complex involved in necrotic cell death.

**Inflammasomes:** The AIM2 and NLRP3 inflammasomes are filamentous assemblies that elicit host defense inside cells by activating caspase-1 for cytokine maturation and cell death.

</div>
