Tetrodotoxin (TTX)

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 46.71%; top: 7.48%; width: 50.83%; height: 43.09%;">

The intestines, ovaries and livers of the **blowfish **contain a poison tetrodotoxin. Tetrodotoxin has been isolated from other widely differing animal species:  octopuses and cuttlefish, toads, newts etc.
TTX is 1200x deadlier than cyanide. A single fish has enough poison to kill 30 people.
The toxin is variously used by animals as a defensive biotoxin to ward off predation, or as both a defensive and predatory venom (e.g., in octopuses, chaetognaths, and ribbon worms).
TTX is a potent blocker of voltage-gated sodium channels 
TTX is a powerful experimental tool to dissect how different ion channels contribute to measures electrical potentials.

</div>

An evolutionary arms race

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 41.44%; top: 10.35%; width: 55.51%; height: 29.93%;">

Newts (*Taricha granulosa*) produce TTX on their skin. TTX production is thought to have evolved to deter vertebrate predators — particularly garter snakes (*Thamnophis sirtalis*). In some populations, garter snakes have evolved resistance to TTX. This resistance is due to amino acid substitutions in the sodium channel proteins (Nav1.4, primarily) of their muscle cells, which reduce the binding affinity of TTX. However, they come with trade-offs: TTX-resistant channels often conduct sodium ions less efficiently, making the snakes slower or less physiologically efficient in other ways.

</div>

![](./lecture_10_electrophysiology/images/slide_53_img_194.png) {left=14.29 top=25.02 width=11.93 height=20.75}

![](./lecture_10_electrophysiology/images/slide_53_img_195.png) {left=26.39 top=25.02 width=13.82 height=20.75}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 42.46%; top: 50.00%; width: 54.49%; height: 43.09%;">

**Hyperkalemic Periodic Paralysis** (HYPP) is an autosomal dominant disease in horses that causes episodes of muscle stiffness, tremors, weakness, and sometimes collapse. It’s caused by a mutation in the voltage-gated sodium channel of skeletal muscle — specifically, the SCN4A gene, which encodes the Nav1.4 sodium channel α-subunit (the same gene is affected in the TTX newt–snake arms race).

HYPP primarily affects American Quarter Horses and related breeds. All affected individuals trace back to a single famous stallion named “Impressive”(he sired 2250 foals), a heavily muscled horse born in 1969. The trait spread because the mutation produced larger, more muscular horses that were favored in halter competitions.

</div>

“Fainting goats” / myotonic goats.

</div>

![](./lecture_10_electrophysiology/images/slide_54_img_197.png) {left=16.02 top=9.54 width=31.17 height=29.10}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 47.67%; top: 7.72%; width: 52.33%; height: 40.16%;">

**Myotonia congenita**  is an autosomal dominant disease in goats characterized by temporary muscle stiffness (**myotonia**) when the animal is startled, excited, or suddenly moves after rest. The muscles lock in a contracted state for 5–20 seconds (no pain, no lasting damage).
Despite their nickname, they don’t truly “faint” — they remain conscious, but their muscles freeze for a few seconds, sometimes causing them to topple over stiffly. The condition is caused by mutations in the **CLCN1 gene**, which encodes the **chloride channel (ClC-1)** in skeletal muscle cell membranes.

</div>

![](./lecture_10_electrophysiology/images/slide_54_img_198.png) {left=20.27 top=40.12 width=22.66 height=57.72}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 47.19%; top: 59.06%; width: 48.44%; height: 26.63%;">

**Action potential recordings from myotonic goat muscle in response to graded electrical stimulation**. 
In the top tracing, stimulation of muscle fibers produces multiple action potential spikes and a small afterdepolarization. With a stronger stimulus, the afterdepolarization reaches threshold voltage and triggers spontaneous action potentials that fire after termination of the electrical stimulus.

There are similar conditions in humans caused by mutations in ion channel genes (for Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, or Cl⁻):
Hyperkalemic periodic paralysis (**HyperPP**) 
Hypokalemic periodic paralysis (**HypoPP**)
**Paramyotonia congenita**
**Myotonia congenita**
**Andersen–Tawil syndrome**


They can cause muscle stiffness, weakness, paralysis, cardiac arrhythmias as well as dysmorphic features (small jaw, low-set ears, clinodactyly).
