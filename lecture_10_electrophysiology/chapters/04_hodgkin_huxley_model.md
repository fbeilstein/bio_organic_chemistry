<div style="position: relative; width: 100%; aspect-ratio: 16 / 9; margin: 0 auto;">

<div style="position: absolute; left: 17.31%; top: 44.66%; width: 77.16%; height: 21.55%;">

The cable equation is not a wave equation; thus, it does not describe signal propagation. Rather, it describes current dissipation due to membrane charging and current leaking. In order for our model to describe wave propagation, we need to account for the fact that membrane resistance has a voltage-dependent component to it. In other words we need to introduce voltage-gated channels. The Hodgkin–Huxley model does that results introducing of one more non-linear, voltage-dependent term.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 17.31%; top: 66.21%; width: 75.45%; height: 14.37%;">

Cable equation describes** passive propagation - **propagation of the signal between** **Ranvier nodes where there are no voltage-gated channels. In order to get a correct model we need to take into account **active propagation - activation and inactivation **of voltage gated channels.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 17.31%; top: 83.00%; width: 75.45%; height: 10.77%;">

Cable equation is still useful as it shows how signal propagation is influenced by the parameters of the axon.

</div>

Action potential conduction

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 13.25%; top: 69.42%; width: 64.07%; height: 32.62%;">

Speed of an action potential: **1-100 m/s**
Potential conduction velocity **increases **with increasing axonal diameter (the squid giant axon can be 1 mm in diameter). Smaller axons require greater depolarization to reach action potential threshold and are more sensitive to being blocked by local anesthetics.
Action potential propagation in myelinated neurons is **faster **than in unmyelinated neurons (less “leaks”).

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 40.25%; top: 3.85%; width: 23.86%; height: 7.18%;">

depolarisation wave

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 68.61%; top: 0.58%; width: 21.49%; height: 60.45%;">

**The squid giant axon** is the very large (up to 1.5 mm in diameter; typically around 0.5 mm) axon that controls part of the water jet propulsion system in squid. It was first described by L. W. Williams in 1909.
While the squid axon is very large in diameter, it is unmyelinated, which decreases the conduction velocity substantially. The conduction velocity of a typical squid axon is about **25 m/s**.
In their Nobel Prize-winning work, Alan Hodgkin and Andrew Huxley performed experiments on the squid giant axon.

</div>

**Demyelination **is a process characterized by the gradual loss of the myelin sheath enveloping nerve fibers. When myelin deteriorates, signal conduction along nerves can be significantly impaired or lost, and the nerve eventually withers. Demyelination may affect both central and peripheral nervous systems, contributing to various neurological disorders such as multiple sclerosis, Guillain-Barré syndrome etc.

<div style="position: absolute; left: 17.70%; top: 39.45%; width: 80.18%; height: 58.48%;">

According to the Hodgkin-Huxley model an action potential involves the following sequence of events. Depolarization of the membrane causes *Na**+* channels to open rapidly (an increase in gNa), resulting in an inward *Na**+* current. This current, by discharging the membrane capacitance, causes further depolarization, thereby opening more *Na**+* channels, resulting in a further increase in inward current. This regenerative process drives the membrane potential toward *E**Na* , causing the rising phase of the action potential. 

The depolarization limits the duration of the action potential in two ways: 
It gradually inactivates the voltage-gated *Na**+ *channels, thus reducing gNa
It opens, with some delay, the voltage-gated *K**+* channels, thereby increasing gK. Consequently, the inward *Na*+ current is followed by an outward *K**+* current that tends to repolarize the membrane.

The combined effect of this transient increase in *K**+** *conductance and the residual inactivation of the* Na**+* channels underlies the **absolute refractory period**, the brief period of time following an action potential when it is impossible to elicit another action potential. As some *K**+* channels begin to close and some *Na**+* channels recover from inactivation, the membrane enters the **relative refractory period**, during which it is possible to trigger an action potential, but only by applying stimuli that are stronger than those normally required to reach threshold.
