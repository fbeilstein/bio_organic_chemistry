The lipid bilayer is largely impermeable to ions.
The intracellular and extracellular concentrations of K+, Na+ and Cl− differ significantly.
Ion channels (both voltage-gated and non–voltage-gated) are selective for specific ions.
An electric potential difference exists across the membrane.
Diffusive and electrical forces together maintain a dynamic equilibrium of ionic fluxes.

**Electrochemical equilibrium.**
If the inside and outside concentrations are different, diffusion tries to make them equal. You need an electric force (equilibrium potential) to oppose diffusion in order maintain concentrations.

**Membrane currents and conductances.** Here is an idealized neuron with sodium potassium pumps (not shown), potassium channels, and sodium channels.

**(a)** Initially, we assume that all channels are closed and the membrane potential equals 0 mV. 





**(b) **Now we open the potassium channels,
and *K**+* flows out of the cell. This movement of *K**+* is an electrical current, *I**K*, and it
flows as long as the membrane conductance to *K**+*, gK>0, and the membrane potential is not equal to the potassium equilibrium potential. 


**(c)** At equilibrium, there is no net potassium current because, although *g**K **>0*, the membrane potential at equilibrium equals *E**K*. At equilibrium, an equal number of* K**+* enters and leaves.

<div style="position: absolute; left: 64.89%; top: 4.12%; width: 34.64%; height: 79.31%;">

**Flipping the membrane potential by changing the relative ionic permeability of the membrane. **
**(a)** The membrane of the idealized neuron. We begin by assuming that the membrane is permeable only to *K* and that *V**m **= E**K**.*


**(b) **We now stipulate that the membrane sodium channels open so that *g**Na**>>g**K* . There is a large driving force on *Na**+*, so *Na**+* rushes into the cell, taking *V**m*  toward *E**Na*.



**(c)** Now we close the sodium channels so that *g**K **>>g**Na*. Because the membrane potential is positive, there is a large driving force on *K**+*. The efflux of *K**+* takes *V**m* back toward *E**K*. 



**(d) **The resting state is restored where *V**m **=** **E**K*.

**(a)** The axon hillock is impaled by two
electrodes, one for recording the membrane potential relative to ground and the other for stimulating the neuron with electrical current.
**(b)** When electrical current is injected into the neuron the membrane is depolarized sufficiently to fire action potentials

<div style="position: absolute; left: 19.26%; top: 1.90%; width: 72.13%; height: 7.18%;">

Action potential is caused by the **depolarization **-  injecting positive charge into a neuron.

</div>

![](./lecture_10_electrophysiology/images/slide_27_img_55.png) {left=17.55 top=44.14 width=37.14 height=50.63}

<div style="position: absolute; left: 60.87%; top: 47.25%; width: 35.62%; height: 49.68%;">

The firing frequency increases with the amount of depolarizing current, there is a limit to the rate at which a neuron can generate action potentials. The maximum firing frequency is about 1000 Hz; once an action potential is initiated, it is impossible to initiate another for about 1 msec. This period of time is called the **absolute refractory period**. 
After absolute refractory period it can be relatively difficult to initiate another action potential for several milliseconds. During this **relative refractory period**, the amount of current required to depolarize the neuron to action potential threshold is elevated above normal.

</div>

The cerebral cortex has two major types of neurons as defined by morphology: **aspinous stellate** cells and **spinypyramidal** cells. 
**(a)** A **stellate **cell typically responds to a steady depolarizing current injected into its soma by firing action potentials at a relatively steady frequency throughout the stimulus.
 

**(b)** Most **pyramidal **cells cannot sustain a steady firing rate. Instead, they fire rapidly at the beginning of the stimulus and then slow down, even if the stimulus remains strong.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 44.44%; top: 68.85%; width: 37.87%; height: 14.37%;">

**(c)** Some cells, including a particular subtype of large pyramidal neuron in the cortex, respond to a steady input with rhythmic, repetitive bursts.
