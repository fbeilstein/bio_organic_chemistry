**Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging** (fMRI) using the Blood Oxygen Level Dependent (**BOLD**) contrast is a noninvasive technique that maps brain activity by detecting changes in blood oxygenation. When a brain region becomes more active, it consumes more oxygen, causing an initial, small drop in oxygenated hemoglobin followed by a surge of blood flow that delivers a surplus of oxygenated hemoglobin. Because oxygenated hemoglobin has different magnetic properties than deoxygenated hemoglobin (**paramagnetic vs diamagnetic**), this difference is measured as a signal change, creating a map of neural activity.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 14.14%; top: 57.04%; width: 82.97%; height: 39.80%;">

**Neural activity**: When a person performs a task, neurons in a specific brain region become active.
**Initial response**: This activity initially increases the demand for oxygen, leading to a slight decrease in oxygenated hemoglobin and a rise in deoxygenated hemoglobin.
**Blood flow increase**: A few seconds later, the brain compensates by increasing blood flow to that region, which delivers more oxygenated hemoglobin than is consumed.
**BOLD signal**: This large increase in oxygenated hemoglobin compared to deoxygenated hemoglobin is what the BOLD technique detects.
**Paramagnetic properties**: Deoxygenated hemoglobin is paramagnetic, meaning it affects the local magnetic field, while oxygenated hemoglobin is not. This difference in magnetic properties allows the fMRI machine to detect the changes in the BOLD signal.

</div>

**Electroencephalography** (EEG) is a non-invasive method to record an electrogram of the electrical activity of the brain. The bio signals detected by EEG have been shown to represent the postsynaptic potentials of pyramidal neurons in the neocortex and allocortex. Limitations: noisy signals and poor resolution for deeper layers.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 13.21%; top: 6.61%; width: 59.81%; height: 21.55%;">

**Magnetoencephalography **(MEG) is a non-invasive medical imaging technique that measures the magnetic fields produced by the brain's electrical activity using a sensitive device called a superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID). It is used to identify the source of brain activity with high temporal resolution (on the order of milliseconds).

</div>
