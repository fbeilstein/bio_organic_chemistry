<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 13.33%; width: 80.35%; height: 77.91%;">

My dog is craving sweets. The problem is that dogs metabolize methylxanthines — especially theobromine and caffeine — poorly. From your biochemistry course, you know that theobromine is abundant in cocoa powder (*Theobroma cacao*) and caffeine is found in coffee, thus present in many sweets. So we should be concerned whether the dog has not ingested too much. 

To estimate a safe limit, you look at veterinary toxicology data (<u>https://www.aspcapro.org/sites/default/files/m-toxbrief_0201.pdf</u>): mild signs of toxicity begin around 20 mg/kg. To stay well below that, you set a conservative safety threshold of 5 mg/kg (25%). Your dog weighs 30 kg. Your dog’s favorite sweets list no more than 3 g/kg of total methylxanthines (the actual amount is expected to be lower). Each portion weighs 15 g. What is the maximum number of portions your dog could safely eat without exceeding your chosen threshold?
