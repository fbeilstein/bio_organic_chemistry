**Problem 1.** Energy carried by a sound wave.

Sound intensity level of a siren at 30 m is LI = 100 dB (<u>source</u>). It is pretty noisy and causes damage from 8 h per day exposure. For how long should the siren blast to emit enough energy for boiling my kettle, e.g. heat 2 L of water from 20 oC to 100 oC? Suppose that specific heat of water is 4.2 kJ/(kg oC).

HINT: sirens are mostly placed on a high ground, so suppose it emits spherical waves and ignore all obstacles.

**Problem 2. **Maximal possible noise.

Calculate the theoretical maximum sound pressure level (SPL) in air (measured in dB). Note that if the amplitude of a pressure wave exceeds atmospheric pressure (≈101,325 Pa), the rarefaction phase (the pressure-drop phase) would drive the pressure below zero — an impossible physical condition. Therefore, atmospheric pressure imposes an upper bound on the amplitude of a sound wave. Keep in mind that the SPL formula uses the root-mean-square pressure pRMS​, not the peak pressure ppeak.
