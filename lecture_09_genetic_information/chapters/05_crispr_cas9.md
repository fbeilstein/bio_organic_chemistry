<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 2.87%; width: 80.35%; height: 95.55%;">

**Cas1–Cas2 are activated by DNA fragments generated from linear (non-circular) DNA, which is a strong signature of invading genetic material.**

Cas1–Cas2 are not triggered by linear DNA directly — rather:
RecBCD/AddAB start processing linear DNA.
Cas1–Cas2 physically interact with these complexes (or with the DNA fragments they produce).
Those small dsDNA fragments are then captured and integrated into the CRISPR array.

RecBCD/AddAB recognize and degrade linear DNA
They bind double-stranded DNA ends.
Foreign linear DNA gets shredded into short fragments — ideal substrates for Cas1–Cas2.
They degrade it until they hit Chi sites, which are abundant in host DNA but absent in invader DNA. So in rare cases when host DNA has ds-break, there is still a way to stop the “autoimmune” process.
