**Bacterial ribosome.**

The 20,000 70S ribosomes (50S + 30S) in a **bacterial **cell constitute nearly a fourth of its mass. Eukaryotes have 80S (60S + 40S). 

Both the 30S and the 50S subunits can be reconstituted in vitro from their constituent proteins and RNA. This reconstitution is an outstanding example of the principle that supramolecular complexes can form spontaneously from their macromolecular constituents.

**All known transfer RNA molecules have the following features:**

Single RNA chain — 73–93 nucleotides (~25 kDa).
L-shaped 3D structure.
Contains 7–15 modified bases (e.g. methylated A, U, C, G) that:
prevent unwanted base pairing,
aid binding to synthetases/ribosome,
fine-tune codon recognition.
~50% base-paired:
4 helical regions forming 2 A-form helices (acceptor + anticodon arms).
5 loops: acceptor stem (3′ CCA), TΨC, D (DHU), variable arm, and anticodon loop.
Combination of helices + loops ensures unique recognition.
5′ end phosphorylated, usually pG.
3′ CCA terminus binds amino acid on the terminal adenosine (acceptor stem).
Anticodon loop at opposite end of L exposes 3 anticodon bases for mRNA pairing.

**Initiation**

The start signal is usually AUG  preceded by several bases that pair with 16S rRNA.

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.91%; top: 40.26%; width: 83.09%; height: 59.74%;">

all known mRNA molecules contain signals that define the beginning and end of each encoded polypeptide chain.
in bacteria, many mRNA molecules are **polycistronic **—that is, they encode two or more polypeptide chains.
initiating codon in mRNA is AUG (methionine) or, less frequently, GUG (valine) or, rarely UUG (leucine).
In addition, each initiator region contains a purine-rich sequence centered about 10 nucleotides on the 5’ side of the initiator codon (**Shine–Dalgarno sequence**).
Two kinds of interactions determine where protein synthesis starts: (1) the pairing of mRNA bases with the 3’ end of 16S rRNA and (2) the pairing of the initiator codon on mRNA with the anticodon of an initiator tRNA molecule.

</div>

</div>

<div style="position: relative; width: 100%; aspect-ratio: 16 / 9; margin: 0 auto;">

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 0.00%; width: 43.07%; height: 100.00%;">

protein synthesis in bacteria starts with the modified amino acid N - formylmethionine (fMet). A special tRNA brings formylmethionine to the ribosome to initiate protein synthesis. This initiator tRNA (abbreviated as tRNAf ) differs from the tRNA that inserts methionine in internal positions (abbreviated as tRNAm).

During processing formylmethionine can be removed.

30S subunit binds IF1 and IF3 → prevents premature 50S joining
F1 binds near A site, directs fMet-tRNAᶠ to P site
IF2–GTP binds fMet-tRNAᶠ → forms 30S initiation complex with mRNA
mRNA positioned via Shine–Dalgarno – 16S rRNA pairing
IF1 and IF3 released → 50S subunit joins
IF2 hydrolyzes GTP → leaves complex

Result: Active 70S initiation complex (rate-limiting step)

</div>

The cycle begins with peptidyl-
tRNA in the P site. 
**(1)** An aminoacyl-tRNA binds in the A site. 
**(2)** With both sites occupied, a new peptide bond is formed.
**(3)** The tRNAs and the mRNA are translocated through the action of elongation factor G, which moves the deacylated tRNA to the E site. 
**(4)** Once there, the tRNA is free to dissociate to complete the cycle.
