**Problem 2. ****Ammonia Toxicity Resulting from an Arginine-Deficient Diet.**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 10.04%; width: 80.35%; height: 87.79%;">

Cats were fasted overnight and then given a single meal complete in all amino acids **except arginine**. Within 2 hours, blood ammonia levels increased from a normal 18 µg/L to 140 µg/L, and the cats showed clinical symptoms of ammonia toxicity. A control group fed a complete amino acid mixture — or a mixture in which arginine was replaced by ornithine — showed no unusual symptoms.
**(a)** What was the role of fasting in the experiment?
**(b)** Why did ammonia levels rise in the experimental group? Why did the absence of arginine lead to ammonia toxicity? 
**(c)** Is arginine an essential amino acid in cats? Why or why not?
**(d)** Why can ornithine be substituted for arginine?

HINTS
Consider whether amino acids can be stored “for later use” in animals.
Think about cyclic metabolic pathways. If you add more substrate at the entry point, does that necessarily increase flux through the cycle? If not, what else must be present? Apply this idea to the urea (ornithine) cycle.
Think about what changes if cats are able to synthesize sufficient arginine on their own.
