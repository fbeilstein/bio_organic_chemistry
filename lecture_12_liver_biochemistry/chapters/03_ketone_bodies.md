**Animals Cannot Convert Fatty Acids Into Glucose**
**Fat stores are abundant**, but **cannot be used to make glucose** when glycogen is depleted.
Critical tissues (e.g., **brain**, **active muscle**) need **glucose**, not acetyl-CoA.
**Biochemical Reason**
**Fatty acids → acetyl-CoA**
**Acetyl-CoA cannot be converted back into pyruvate or oxaloacetate**
Pyruvate → acetyl-CoA is **irreversible**
No pathway exists in animals for **acetyl-CoA → pyruvate/OAA**
**Citric Acid Cycle Limitation**
Acetyl-CoA enters the **citric acid cycle**
But:
**2 carbons enter as acetyl-CoA**
**2 carbons leave as CO₂** (via isocitrate DH and α-ketoglutarate DH)
Oxaloacetate is **regenerated**, not created **de novo** → **No net gain of OAA**, so **no net synthesis of glucose**
**Contrast With Plants**
Plants have additional enzymes (glyoxylate cycle) → allow **conversion of acetyl-CoA into oxaloacetate** → animals do **not**, so they **cannot** turn fats into glucose.

**Formation of ketone bodies.** The ketone bodies—acetoacetate, D-3-
hydroxybutyrate, and acetone—are formed from acetyl CoA primarily in the **liver**. Enzymes catalyzing these reactions are 
**(1)** 3-ketothiolase, 
**(2)** hydroxymethylglutaryl CoA synthase, 
**(3)** hydroxymethylglutaryl CoA cleavage enzyme, and 
**(4)** D-3-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase. Acetoacetate spontaneously decarboxylates to form acetone.

Ketone bodies can be regarded as a water-soluble, transportable form of acetyl units. Fatty acids are released by adipose tissue and converted into acetyl units by the liver, which then exports them as acetoacetate. As might be expected, acetoacetate also has a regulatory role. High levels of acetoacetate in the blood signify an abundance of acetyl units and lead to a decrease in the rate of lipolysis in adipose tissue.

**Ketone Bodies**

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 16.07%; top: 7.68%; width: 40.57%; height: 92.32%;">

**Major Producers & Types**
Produced in **liver mitochondria**:
**Acetoacetate**, **3-Hydroxybutyrate**, **Acetone**
Exported from liver → diffuse into **blood** → used by **extrahepatic tissues**
**Physiological Role**
**Heart** and **renal cortex** preferentially use **acetoacetate** over glucose
**Brain** normally uses glucose but switches to ketone bodies during **starvation/diabetes**
In prolonged starvation: **~75% of brain energy** from ketone bodies
Ketone bodies = **water-soluble “acetyl-unit transport form”**
**Utilization in Tissues**
**Acetoacetate → acetoacetyl-CoA**
Via **CoA transferase** (absent in liver → liver cannot use ketones)
**Acetoacetyl-CoA → 2 acetyl-CoA** (thiolase → enters TCA cycle)
**3-Hydroxybutyrate → acetoacetate + NADH** → processed as above

</div>

<div style="position: absolute; left: 60.52%; top: 10.08%; width: 39.69%; height: 85.48%;">

**Metabolic Regulation**
High acetoacetate levels ↓ **lipolysis** in adipose tissue (feedback signal of abundant acetyl units)
**Pathology: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)**
**Insulin deficiency →**
Liver cannot absorb glucose → insufficient oxaloacetate
Adipose tissue releases excess FAs → massive ketogenesis
Result: **high ketone levels **(0.9 vs 0.03 mg/mL in blood, 5 vs 0.125 g/24h urinary excretion), **acidosis**, impaired CNS function
**Therapeutic Note**
**Ketogenic diets** (high fat, low carb) induce mild ketosis
Used clinically in **drug-resistant childhood epilepsy** (mechanism unclear)
